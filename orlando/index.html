Water Damage We See Most in Orlando

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common Orlando calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: Nothing is more time-critical than a broken water line, which keeps pouring until the supply is cut. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Rain running into the house at ground level ranks a close second. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In Orlando kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Dampness in the space below the floor can wait a few days, though not a few months. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. Avoid wiping or bleaching a big area on your own, as that sends spores into other rooms. How Long Drying Takes in an Orlando Home

A surface that feels dry is not proof that a room is dry. The face of drywall often seems normal when the cavity side is damp, and carpet fibers dry long before the padding underneath. That is why professional drying runs on instruments. Our Orlando technicians carry probe meters for wood and wallboard, scanning meters that read through finishes, and infrared cameras that pick up damp areas as cooler spots.

Not everything dries at the same rate. Drywall and carpet give up moisture fast, but studs, subflooring, hardwood and concrete are slow. We place air movers and dehumidifiers according to those differences and adjust them as the daily readings change. A span of 3 to 5 days covers most jobs, and we will tell you early if yours looks like an exception.

The expensive error is pulling equipment too soon. Damp materials sealed behind new finishes may mold, buckle or delaminate months afterward. So for every Orlando job we dry to a measured target and keep the readings on file.
