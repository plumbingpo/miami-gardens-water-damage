Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Miami Gardens

A flooded crawl space rarely announces itself. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. At that point the batts are typically saturated and the framing overhead has sat damp for several days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Once the standing water is gone, the job is only halfway done. Because lumber under a floor is slow to soak and slow to dry, our crew tracks its moisture content with a meter and runs dehumidification for the 3 to 5 days it normally takes to bring the readings down. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • An increase in insects and pests that like damp, dark spaces
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • Flooring overhead that seems chilly, clammy, spongy or springy underfoot
  • Corroded metal such as duct straps, pipe hangers and steel posts
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Dampness Rises Indoors

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

After a week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Expect softer framing, rusted fasteners, and a wet environment that draws in termites and similar pests.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Safety Check and Inspection

We check for electrical hazards, sagging ducts and contaminated water before anyone crawls in. Next comes charting the extent of the water and metering the joists and subfloor for moisture.

2

Remove Standing Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. While pumping, the crew also cleans off the sediment the water left on the plastic.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Final Drying and Checks

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Electrical and Duct Hazards

Under a typical house you will find cable runs, electrical boxes and duct lines. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Settling Under the House

Saturated soil can soften around footings and let piers settle or lean. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Termites and Other Pests

A wet crawl space offers the soft wood and moisture that wood destroying insects and mice prefer. Drying the space removes much of what attracts them.
